A claim the firm resolved this fall turned on a lesson worth institutionalizing. A client's written carve-out of a claim from our engagement was expressly limited in time - it ended on a stated date. The carve-out was perfect; the follow-through was not. The review tickler was closed on the client's assurance that an extension "would be confirmed," the confirmation never came, the client's general counsel departed, and the date passed in silence.

New rule, effective today, in the docketing manual at 4.7: any scope limitation with an expiry date, condition, or review date gets two independent docket entries - the review date and a fallback fourteen days before expiry - and neither may be closed except on receipt of a client writing that resolves the question. An assurance that paper is coming is not paper.

A carve-out without an end date is a wall. A carve-out with an end date is a gate, and gates need gatekeepers.

Evelyn Stanhope
