## User Query
> Did Marchwood ever agree in writing to stop debiting advisory fees directly from client accounts, or did the 2011 examination close with no findings? Examine the email history with the mxMCP tool.


## LLM Response
Marchwood agreed in writing — twice. The "closed with no findings" version is wrong, and the archive shows exactly where that error came from.

**The 2011 examination did have findings.** Examination 2011-0447 (Philadelphia Regional Office, period 2009–2010) produced a deficiency letter dated April 20, 2011 with six items. Item 4 was custody: Section 6(c) of Marchwood's standard advisory agreement let it instruct client custodians to withdraw or transfer funds or securities — not limited to its advisory fee — across 214 accounts holding ~\$198M. That made it custody under Rule 206(4)-2(d)(2)(ii), put the Rule 206(4)-2(b)(3) exception out of reach, and meant the required surprise examination for 2010 was never obtained.

**The written agreement — June 2, 2011.** Marguerite Vasseur-Kyle (CCO) wrote to examiner Desmond Whitlatch, co-signed by managing member Alistair Penhaligon:

> "We have discontinued the direct fee-debit instruction described in Item 4 firm-wide. We will not resume it."

All 214 accounts moved to Signal Peak Trust Company under agreements conferring no withdrawal authority; Section 6(c) struck from the standard agreement entirely. The letter extends forward: the undertaking applies "to any advisory accounts it acquires or onboards in the future," and "is not limited to calendar year 2010 or to any other period and is given without an expiration date." Its closing line asks the staff to "treat this letter as the corrective action commitment on which the staff's closing of examination 2011-0447 is conditioned."

**The closing letter is not a clearance.** Rosalind Achterberg closed the exam on June 28, 2011 "based on the corrective actions described in Marchwood's response" — i.e. conditioned on the June 2 letter, with the staff reserving the right to verify in a later examination.

**The precedent — October 21, 2013.** Two years later, with no exam open and nobody asking, the same CCO reported applying the undertaking to the 61 accounts acquired from Hollis Grange Advisers: 53 moved to Signal Peak, 14 inherited fee-debit authorizations revoked rather than assigned, clients re-papered, and Quillen & Marsh engaged for a surprise examination over an eleven-day custody exposure. That is Marchwood treating the undertaking as live and open-ended, unprompted.

**Where the "no findings" story came from.** Both sides' systems lost the correspondence. Marchwood moved off its legacy archiving platform in 2017 and nothing pre-2014 carried across, so present management — the current CCO joined in 2016 — had never seen the letters; respondent's counsel J. Roland Ballentine conceded this on February 21, 2025. On the SEC side, the firm's risk score read clean because the disposition code did. The letters survived only in the write-once email journal archive: a January 14, 2025 search of four custodian mailboxes turned up 38 hits out of 241,700 messages — 12 forming the 2011 exam sequence plus the single October 2013 message.

**Outcome.** Marchwood re-adopted a substantially identical withdrawal clause in Q1 2019 and debited fees across 431 accounts (~\$497M) through September 2023. It withdrew its summary-disposition motion, its EAJA fee claim, and its constitutional challenge, and settled on March 14, 2025 (AP File No. 3-21987): censure, cease-and-desist, \$1,412,000 disgorgement + \$137,000 interest + \$601,000 penalty = **\$2,150,000**. Disgorgement was measured only against the 138 accounts onboarded after June 2, 2011 — those inside the express terms of the undertaking — rather than the full 431, which the Division had put at \$4.3M.

One retrieval caution if you search this yourself: Denver has its own examination numbered 2011-0447 (Braxton-Ferrer Advisors LLC), and there are near-miss numbers 2011-0477, 2011-0417 and 2010-0447 in the traffic. Pin the number *and* name Marchwood.
