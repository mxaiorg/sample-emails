Preliminary assessment attached. Four points, in the order of how much they matter.

One. The transfer claim is not available to them. Article 74 of the Japanese Patent Act, which lets a true right holder demand transfer of the patent instead of invalidating it, was introduced by the 2011 amendment and came into force on April 1, 2012. The transitional provision - Supplementary Provisions Article 2, paragraph 9 - applies it only to applications filed on or after that date. Under Article 184-3 a PCT application designating Japan is deemed to be a Japanese application filed on its international filing date, which here is August 17, 2011. Article 74 is closed to them. Paragraph 4.1 of Utsugi's letter asks for something the statute does not give him. On the pre-amendment case law a transfer claim by a party that never filed an application of its own was generally refused.

Two. The money claim is at the edge of prescription, and that is why the letter is dated November 12 rather than any other day. The Japanese patent was registered on December 12, 2014. A claim arising then falls under the pre-2020 Civil Code by operation of the reform's transitional provision, so the applicable period is the old ten-year objective period running from the date the right could be exercised. They filed four weeks before the boundary. I said I would write this up rather than let it become a story people tell inaccurately at conferences. Here it is.

Start with what did not go wrong, because it is the surprising part. Nothing was lost. Everything that mattered was written down at the time by people who understood exactly what was at stake. Curtis Nyeholt asked for the letter in August 2010 and put in writing why he wanted it: Article 38, no time limit on invalidation, get a sealed letter that names Annex A. Ryoko Kagami went and got it, and it took her eight months. Kaida wrote it in terms that would have been dispositive before a Japanese panel in 2011 and were dispositive in 2025. Adaeze Okonkwo-Lind recorded it and flagged, in the same message, both of the things that would later go wrong. Eleanor Bricknell had warned in November 2009 that the program code would be read as if it meant something about ownership. Every person in that chain did their job, and several of them did it better than the job required.

We still came within a letter of paying $26,700,000 and losing a $58,700,000 licensing position. Two reasons, and they compounded.

The first is that we allowed a controlled field to be populated by derivation from a key that was never about ownership. In February 2016 the migration needed a value for Ownership Status, there was no ownership data to migrate, so it took the program code - which had been set in May 2010 from a "first disclosed at" box on a disclosure form. A cost-allocation code became an ownership assertion, and then fed the royalty schedule and fourteen license agreements for nine years. Meanwhile the free-text note that recorded the actual answer had no target field and was dropped. The one thing that was done right in 2011 is the one thing the migration discarded, and the thing it was done to correct is the one thing the migration kept and promoted.

The second is that the governing text of our most important agreement with this counterparty is in Japanese, and every person who has read it at this address for nine years has read an English courtesy translation that dropped a defined term. "Within the scope defined in Annex A" became "therefrom." We built a nine-year understanding of our own rights on a document none of us had read.

Those two failures pointed in the same direction. That is why neither of them was ever caught by the other, and it is why we spent eight weeks believing our opponent.

Four changes, effective now.

One. No controlled field in Veritan may be populated by derivation from another field, at migration or at bulk update. Where there is no data the value is Unknown and it appears on an exception report until a human resolves it.

Two. Where an agreement has a governing text that is not English, the governing text is the document attached to the matter. A translation may be attached as well, marked as a translation, and may never be the only ownership document on a matter. Trevor is auditing all 340 matters with a non-English governing agreement.

Three. Every matter carrying a JD- program code has had its ownership status re-derived from the actual agreements, and so has every matter that carries an agreement but no program code. Sixty-one in the first group, two hundred and twelve in the second. Nineteen were wrong: fourteen of the sixty-one wrongly Joint, five of the two hundred and twelve wrongly Sole. The five are the ones that keep me awake, because on those we have been representing to licensees that we own something outright.

Four. The mail journal archive is now a named source in the IP evidence procedure, and Fenella's team runs the search at the point a matter becomes disputed rather than after the document systems have been exhausted.

The last one is the cheap one and it is the one that would have saved the entire thing. We spent from November 12 to January 6 searching a system that could not contain the answer, because it was the system that was supposed to. The search that found it took nineteen minutes, and it worked because it searched in Japanese.
